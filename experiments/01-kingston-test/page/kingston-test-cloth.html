    What the test shows

    The models add something the keywords don't. Of the 50 links across a century, 28 rank outside the keyword method's top 50. In the cloth, the keyword version keeps 31 of the 65 raised blocks, and a shuffled version keeps about 12. Take the models away and about half the cloth's structure changes, which passes your removal test at this small size.

    The model-only links gather around a few actions. A sacred building put to secular use: the bridge chapel as a lock-up, the Saxon church as a cottage and school. A repair paid for by appeal: Town Bridge alms in 1400, Avoncliff weir in 2020. Something buried found during other work: the villa under a football pitch, burials under a boiler house. A closure that empties the town: the bank failure of 1841, the works closing in 1992. These are interpretive affinities. None is a documented connection.

    Rubber hardly crosses time. Most rubber records link to other rubber records within 150 years, so the century rule drops them. The affinity your thesis uses as its example, river management with machine tension and rubber absorbing force, never came up.

    Watch what the sources are. Many records come from heritage listings, which describe buildings through their alterations, conversions and repairs. The conversion and repair links may say more about how listings are written than about the town. Older texts in other voices would test that.

    The two readers aren't independent. Opus and Sonnet agreed on 63% of their links, but they come from the same family, and a Claude model also wrote the paraphrases they read.

    How it was made

    1. Gathering. Claude research agents collected 46 records from 34 web pages by about 20 publishers. Every record is a paraphrase written by Claude, because the sources are in copyright. A separate agent checked each record against its page; seven had small slips, now fixed and marked below.
    2. Dating. Each record keeps its date written and the date it describes. For the cloth, each record also has one focal year that I chose, such as 1300 for the Town Bridge's 13th-century arches. Those choices decide the row order.
    3. Blind reading. The records were shuffled and given neutral codes. Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Sonnet 5.5 read them separately, with no project context, and answered the prompt below.
    4. Keyword baseline. TF-IDF word overlap with common English words removed, run on the same text the models read.
    5. The century rule. A link counts only between records at least 100 years apart that come from different pages. That leaves 740 of the 1,035 possible pairs.
    6. The weave. Each place is a block of eight warp threads; each record is four picks of weft, coloured by its strand. A record's own place is woven as satin, so it reads as solid warp. Elsewhere, its strongest link to that place sets the structure: 3/1 twill for 3 or more, 2/2 twill for 2 to 2.5, plain weave for anything weaker, and weft-faced twill for no link. The keyword and shuffled cloths raise the same number of blocks at each level, ranked by their own scores.
    For every passage, list up to 4 OTHER passages it is most strongly related to. Give each a strength from 1 (faint) to 5 (strong) and a reason of at most 8 words. Do not count a resemblance if it rests only on: the same place or building, the same named person or organisation, or the same historical period. Any other kind of resemblance counts. If a passage has nothing that meets this, list fewer or none — don't pad.

    Limits

    • No embedding model. Model downloads are blocked in the workspace this ran in, so the models judged the records in words. That is a generated reading, not a measurement, and the reasons are not a view of how either model decided.
    • The models read the records before the seven fact-check fixes. The fixes changed wording, not substance.
    • Forty-six records is a small sample, and none is yet in its original wording.
