# Inspirations

Five practices this project looks to. They are named here as influences to learn from, not sources to reproduce: nothing in this repository copies their code, patterns or imagery.

The descriptions of each practice are taken from the artists' own sites (read 13 October 2026). The "what this project takes from it" notes are a first draft by Claude and should be replaced with James's own words.

## Anna Lucia

<https://annalucia.io/>

Berlin-based artist "working with computer code and textile, exploring the intersection of algorithmic systems and traditional craft."

Works that sit closest to this project:

- **Loom** (2021): a long-form generative algorithm emulating a four-shaft weaving loom according to a digital punching card.
- **Generations** (2023): a collaboration with the Gee's Bend Quiltmakers, translating their legacy of improvisation and material reuse into a generative algorithm.
- **33 Million** (2024): digital works and embroideries from an algorithm rearranging patterns of historical needlework samplers.
- **Oefenstof** (2025): machine embroideries on the relationship between cross-stitch samplers and classical computational patterns.
- **Patched Paradise** (2023): treats code as a material, and works the tension between computational structure and the intuitive logic of textile craft.

What this project takes from it: code and cloth as one practice, where the algorithm ends in a made object and the craft's own logic (a draft, a sampler, a punched card) is the notation. The thesis already names her as the reference for computation, repetition, variation and material, and is clear that the aim is to learn from that relationship, not to reproduce her patterns or assume this work must be woven.

## Patrik Hübner

<https://www.patrik-huebner.com/>

Generative designer and creative technologist. He builds identity systems that respond to data and context, and describes his role as joining "the vision of a Creative Director" with "the logic of a Creative Technologist".

Works that sit closest to this project:

- **BRUTE**: an evolving wine brand shaped by wind, rain and sun.
- **Philharmonie Luxembourg**: a dynamic identity that responds to live music.
- **Where There Is Data, There Is Design** and his **Data-Design Dictionary**.
- His book, *The Generative Mind*.

What this project takes from it: data as the thing that shapes form, with a stated mapping from one to the other, so that a change in the source makes an intelligible change in the output. That is the thesis's requirement that "similar measured relationships should produce corresponding formal behaviour".

## Matt DesLauriers

<https://www.mattdesl.com/>

Canadian artist based in the UK, whose practice focuses on "code, software, and generative processes". Also a prolific open source contributor who builds tools for creative coding and generative art.

Works that sit closest to this project:

- **Pattern Language** (2024): a generative artwork and installation inspired by Åkle weaving patterns in Balestrand, Norway.
- **Meridian** (2021–2022): a long-form generative artwork and book.
- **Subscapes** (2021): the project and his three-part essay about making it.
- **The Sferic Project** (2023–ongoing): art and research on Earth's natural radio noise.

What this project takes from it: working in the open, with the tools and the write-up shared alongside the art; seeded, reproducible systems where one algorithm yields a family of related works; and output made for print and plotter as well as screen. Pattern Language is also a precedent for a generative work rooted in one place's weaving tradition.

## Zach Lieberman

<http://zach.li/>

Artist and educator in New York. "I create artwork with code, and focus on building experimental drawing and animation tools... My main focus is how computation can be used as medium for poetry." Co-founder of the School for Poetic Computation and co-creator of openFrameworks, the open source C++ toolkit for creative coding. He has posted a software sketch daily for years.

Works that sit closest to this project:

- **Daily sketches**, and his writing on the practice.
- **Land Lines**: an experiment in drawing and geography.
- **openFrameworks** and **SFPC**, as open tools and open teaching.

What this project takes from it: the sketch as the unit of work. Make many small studies, quickly, and let the body of work show what deserves to grow. The `studies/` folder follows that habit. And the idea that code can be poetic about its subject, which is the gap this project has to cross: from a diagram of a town's records to something felt.

## Giorgia Lupi

<https://giorgialupi.com/>

Information designer and partner at Pentagram, where she leads an information design team. She calls her approach Data Humanism: "an approach that reclaims the human side of data."

Her manifesto, *Data Humanism* (first published in Print magazine), sets out principles that speak directly to this project:

- **Embrace complexity.** "It is impractical to avoid a certain level of visual complexity." Layered, dense work invites slower and deeper reading.
- **Move beyond standards.** "One size does not fit all." Draw with the data to find "what is unique about the numbers at hand."
- **Sneak context in, always.** "Data is a tool that filters reality in a highly subjective way."
- **Remember that data is imperfect, as we are.** "Data is primarily human-made." Show uncertainty and error instead of implying truth.

Works that sit closest to this project:

- **Dear Data**, the project her manifesto uses to show how subjective data collection is.
- **My Life with Long Covid** ("1374 Days"): a visual essay for The New York Times that counts a stretch of time day by day.
- **In Defense of the Detour** (2026): a visual essay for The New York Times on AI and the creative process.
- **& Other Stories**: a fashion collection driven by data.

What this project takes from it: every data point is somebody's small, imperfect account of something, and deserves its own mark with its own detail. For this corpus that means showing what a record actually says about time (a vague span, a broken end, a single year) instead of flattening it to a dot, and letting the density of that detail be what rewards looking closely. Added at James's suggestion after study 04, which he found had lost "all of the sense of data and connection and differentiation".

## On influence and originality

This project is partly a response to the feeling that all AI does is copy. The honest position, from the thesis: "An original artwork does not, by itself, settle the ethics or provenance of every model used to make it." What the repository can do is make its own process inspectable: every source is cited, every prompt and model judgement is kept, and every visual rule is written down where it is used.
